This Week's Muffin · Peach-Cornmeal, and I Have Feelings

Well now. This week the good people behind the counter went and made a peach-cornmeal muffin, and I want you all to sit down before I tell you how I feel about it, because I feel a great deal.

The cornmeal is the thing. It gives the whole affair a little grit — not in a bad way, mind you, but in the way a good porch has a little grit on it. Honest. The peach shows up soft and generous, like it isn't in any hurry, and there's a hum of something warm underneath. Vanilla, maybe. I asked and nobody would confirm. That's fine. A cat keeps some mysteries.

Roy would have loved this one. He was a man who fussed over his beehives every summer — waxed and patient, always coming in smelling faintly of clover — and he believed peaches were nature's way of apologizing for the heat. He'd have eaten two and pretended it was one. I knew his tricks. I let him have them.

Now, my one quibble, and I say this only because I love this place: the tops could stand to be a touch craggier. A muffin top ought to have a little architecture to it, a little cliff face. These came out gentle and rounded, like they'd been smoothed over by a committee. My cousin Frederick, who insists he once catered something famous, would call that "a missed opportunity." Frederick exaggerates. But not this time.

Still — warm, buttery, and it pairs beautifully with a gray Sunday and the low mutter of the radio. Eugene watched me eat mine from his usual corner and said nothing, which from Eugene is high praise. There was a new little fellow in the doorway too, a kitten I'm calling Mortimer until told otherwise, and he seemed impressed by the smell alone.

Get one before they're gone. Tell them Doris sent you. They'll know exactly what that means, and they'll pretend they don't.

—Doris
