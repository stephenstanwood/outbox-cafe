This Week's Muffin · Plum-Cardamom, and a Word About Restraint

The plum-cardamom came back this week, which is how I know the season has turned regardless of what the thermometer in the bay window claims. That thermometer has been wrong since the spring. I have stopped consulting it and started consulting the muffin case, which has never once lied to me.

Let me say plainly that this is a good muffin. The plums were actual plums — dark ones, the kind that go a little jammy at the edges and leave a purple stain in the crumb like a watercolor accident. Whoever is doing the baking did not strain them into a paste. They left them in pieces. That is a decision, and it was the correct one.

The cardamom, however. We must talk about the cardamom. There is a version of this muffin, and I have had it in years past, where the cardamom sits behind the plum and waves politely. This week it came out front and did a whole number. My cousin Frederick — who claims to have been an extra in something, and who I do not press on the details — has a theory that cardamom is a spice best used the way you'd use a comma. Sparingly, and never three in a row. I think about that more than I'd like to admit.

Roy used to put cardamom in his plum jam every autumn, and every autumn I'd tell him he'd gone too far, and every autumn he'd say "Doris, you'll thank me in February." And I did, generally. So perhaps I am not the neutral party here. Perhaps I am carrying some old business into this muffin case. That's allowed. That's most of what a Sunday is for.

Eugene has taken up a new position near the corkboard, closer to the radiator, which I take as a forecast. The radio was playing something with a great deal of trumpet in it and nobody turned it down, and I did not ask them to.

Verdict: buy the plum-cardamom. Get a coffee that can stand up to it. Sit near the window and let the spice do whatever it's going to do.

—Doris
