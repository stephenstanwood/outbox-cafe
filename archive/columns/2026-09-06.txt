This Week's Muffin · Chocolate-Zucchini, and the Question of Honesty

The chocolate-zucchini muffin has returned, as it does every year around the end of August, when somebody's garden gets away from them and the rest of us are asked to be gracious about it. I have been coming here long enough to recognize a vegetable in a disguise. I want that on the record before I say anything nice.

Now. It is good. I am as surprised as anyone. The crumb is dark and damp in the way you want and stubbornly refuse to admit you want, and there is enough cocoa in it that the zucchini is doing structural work rather than flavor work, which is the correct assignment for zucchini. My cousin Frederick, who will tell you he was in something once, took a bite and said "oh" in a tone I have not heard from him since the year of the good pears. That is a review.

My late husband Roy went through a period of making jam out of anything that would sit still, and one summer it was zucchini, with lemon and far too much ginger. He called it a preserve. I called it a warning. But he understood the principle, which is that a garden gives you what it gives you and the only sin is wasting it. I think about that when I see a muffin trying this hard.

Two small notes, offered in love. The chocolate chips have migrated to the bottom, which happens, but a light dusting of flour on them beforehand would fix it and I will say no more. And they are cut a touch large this week. I watched a kitten named Mortimer attempt one on Thursday and it was, frankly, a hostage situation. He got there. It took commitment.

The corkboard has a notice about somebody's lost umbrella, navy, wooden handle. I have not seen it. Eugene has not seen it either, and he sees a great deal from where he sits.

Rain expected midweek. Get one before the garden runs out and we are all back to blueberry, which is fine, which has always been fine, which is exactly the problem with fine.

—Doris
