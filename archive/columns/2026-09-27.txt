This Week's Muffin · Plum-Cardamom, and the Question of Restraint

The plum-cardamom muffins arrived on Tuesday, which is a Tuesday sort of thing to do. I was in the bay window, as I am, and I watched the tray come out and I thought: well. Somebody has been reading.

Here is my honest assessment. The plum is excellent. It is a late-September plum, the kind that has given up on being pretty and committed instead to being good, and it goes soft in the crumb in a way that makes the whole top of the muffin sink just slightly toward the center. I approve of a sunken top. A muffin that stands at full attention is usually hiding something.

The cardamom, however, has opinions of its own, and I am not sure the two of them were properly introduced. There is a moment — the second bite, usually — where the cardamom steps forward and says something and the plum has to sit there and take it. Roy used to do the same thing with vanilla. He made jam every September, a whole weekend of it, jars lined up on the sill like a little congregation, and one year he put vanilla in the plum and I said, Roy, the plum was doing fine. He said he wanted to give it a friend. That is the difference between us. He was generous and I am correct.

I ran into Frederick by the corkboard and he said the muffins were "cinematic," which is the sort of thing he says. He has been saying it since the eighties. Eugene had no comment, but Eugene rarely does, and I respect that in a spider.

The radio was playing something with a lot of strings in it and the rain came in around four and honestly, with the rain, the cardamom made more sense. Perhaps that is the note I want to leave. This is a muffin for weather. Do not eat it on a bright day. It will seem like too much. Wait for Thursday.

I will be here.

—Doris
