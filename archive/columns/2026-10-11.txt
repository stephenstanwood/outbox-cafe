This Week's Muffin · Savory Sweet Potato-Sage, and the Case for Being Surprised

I will admit I approached this week's muffin with suspicion. A muffin, in my experience, is a sweet thing. It is a small cake that has agreed to behave itself at breakfast. So when I saw "Savory Sweet Potato-Sage" chalked on the board, I sat very still in the bay window for some time and considered my position.

Here is where I have landed. It is good. It is, in fact, very good. The sweet potato keeps it moist all the way through, which is more than I can say for certain bran muffins of years past. The sage is fried crisp and pressed into the top, so you get a little crackle before anything else. There is a bit of sharp cheese in there, I believe, and a pinch of black pepper that sneaks up on you around the third bite. My only complaint is that the bottoms run a touch pale. Another few minutes in the oven would not hurt anybody.

It tastes like mid-October, is what I am trying to say. It tastes like the leaves outside going orange and the radiator clanking on for the first time. My cousin Frederick would call it "rustic," but Frederick calls everything rustic, including his own whiskers.

My late husband Roy used to sit on the porch every autumn morning with his eyes on the feeder, waiting for the juncos to come back. He said the birds you don't expect are always the best ones. I thought of him with this muffin. I did not expect it, and I was glad.

So my advice is this. Order the thing you are unsure about at least once a season. Eugene, the lobby spider, has never once hesitated, and he seems very content. Also, please, a little longer in the oven.

—Doris
