This Week's Muffin · Peach Cornmeal, and a Word About Restraint

The peach cornmeal muffin arrived Tuesday morning without any announcement, which is how the good ones always arrive. No sign on the corkboard, no fuss. Just a tray in the case and the smell doing the advertising.

I want to be careful here, because I have been accused of enthusiasm. But this is the best thing to come out of that kitchen since the spring. The cornmeal gives it a bit of grit — and I mean that as the highest compliment. A muffin should have something to say for itself. Too many of them are just cake wearing a paper cup and hoping nobody asks questions. This one has texture. It has a little crunch on the top from whatever they scattered up there, and the peaches are actual peaches, cut in pieces you can identify, not stirred into oblivion.

My late husband Roy would have gone in for this one. Roy put up peaches every August in the back kitchen, and the whole apartment smelled like syrup for a week and I complained about it every single year, and I would very much like to complain about it again. He always said you should be able to see the fruit. He was right about that and about very little else, which is a joke he would have appreciated.

Now. My one criticism, and I offer it in the spirit of improvement: they are underfilled. I don't know who is scooping, but they are scooping timidly. There is a good half inch of unclaimed real estate at the top of that paper cup. My cousin Frederick, who is not a serious person, said maybe that's on purpose. It is not on purpose. It is caution, and caution has no business in a muffin tin.

Eugene has been reworking his corner of the lobby all week, which I take as a sign of confidence in the season. The rain came through Thursday and made everything smell like the ground again. Good weather for cornmeal, if you ask me.

Get the peach one. Get two, given the portioning.

—Doris
