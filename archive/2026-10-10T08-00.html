The Basic Step & the Turn

Count it with me, out loud, I do not care who is listening at the counter.

  1. Gentlemen! LEFT foot FORWARD — one! Right foot forward — two! Left foot to the SIDE — three! Close the right — four! Ladies, you are doing all of this backwards and in heels, so you are already the better dancer.
  2. Left forward — five! Right foot OUT — six! Close the left and give your partner the HIP-CHECK — SEVEN! A little bump. A wink with the hips. Then step the right foot back and — EIGHT — the CALABASH FREEZE.
  3. And off we go again! Left forward — one! Right — two! Cross the left over to the right — three! (See the ERRATUM. I will speak to the printer.) Right foot round, the turn begins — four!
  4. Keep turning — five, six! Now the SPIN — seven! — and plant it — EIGHT, TA-DA! Did you fall over? Wonderful! Falling over is the second lesson!

The Calabash Freeze

On count EIGHT of the fourth bar, the gentleman stops dead, straightens his back and balances an invisible calabash of palm-wine on his head. He holds it for the whole of the trumpet riff. The lady looks at him as if to say, “Don’t you dare spill it.” That is the name of the song. That is the whole joke. It is a very good joke.

ANYBODY who does the calabash freeze on beat 6 will be asked to sit down.

Three Variations for Show-Offs

1. The Double Calabash. Gentleman, on the next “one”, hand the calabash to the lady. Ladies, balance it. If either of you drops an invisible calabash, apologise to the floor.

2. The Trumpet Point. When the trumpets go ta-DAH!, point at them. Mr. Ofori and Mr. Badu are the finest trumpet section on any roof in this city. They can hold a note longer than a queue at the post office. They deserve your finger.

3. The Corridor Spin. Make the final spin a double. Most couples will need to practise in the corridor first. I have spoken to the hotel. The corridor is AVAILABLE.

If Your Partner Is Taller Than You

Gentlemen whose lady is taller: rejoice! Her calabash sits higher than yours and you simply look up at it with admiration, which is correct. Ladies whose gentleman is taller: on the spin he raises his arm higher. That is HIS job. Do not stand on a chair.

A Stern Note About the Cables

The band’s cables run along the front of the stage. They belong to the amplifier. The amplifier belongs to me. It cost more than your shoes. Shuffle AROUND them. Anybody found shuffling ON them will be walked to the punch bowl and made to stand beside it until the interval.

Trouble With Your Feet?

Wonderful! That means you can HEAR the music. Press PRACTISE SLOWLY and the band will play at half speed. They will grumble. They are paid.

What speed is my gramophone?
This is a 45, my friend. But if your auntie’s machine only does 33, set the switch to 33 and dance like a very relaxed person. I allow it.
Is the trumpet riff the same every time?
Every second bar, two notes, exactly. If you hear three, that is Mr. Badu showing off and I will speak to him.
