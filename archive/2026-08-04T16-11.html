The thread, since you asked

It's cotton. Six-cord, plain, off a reel that cost nothing. Then it sat in the yard on Tuesday while it got run through rice paste and powdered glass and pulled through a palm three times to dry, and that is the entire secret, so stop looking at me like it's a trick.

It is not strong. Snap a length with two fingers — go on, do it, I'm not precious about it. It cuts because it saws. Sawing needs movement. So a line that is standing still is not defending itself, it is a surface. It is something for a moving line to eat through, at leisure, while you stand there with your jaw set feeling brave.

Say it back to me: the one who gives, cuts.

It has no tail, and that's the point

A tail makes a kite behave. A tail makes it sit where you put it like a dog. We do not want it behaving. We want it able to turn on the spot and go, which means it is unstable, permanently, by design, on purpose, deliberately.

Its nose points wherever the tension last told it to point. Slack, and the nose falls off sideways and it starts slipping and turning and looking for the ground. One sharp pull and it snaps upright and shoots forward like it's been shoved. That's not steering. That's you telling it something down a hundred metres of string and hoping it's still true when it gets there.

A hundred metres is a weight

You keep flicking your wrist like there's a wire between you. There isn't. There's a long soft heavy curve of wet-ish thread hanging in the air with its own belly in it, and every single thing you ask for has to travel down that belly.

Short line: it answers you. Long line: you ask, you count to two, then it answers, and by then you've asked three more times and it's doing all four at once and going over on its back.

Feel the sag. When it's slack you can see it, that curve, that's your delay drawn in the air. When you haul it, it goes dead straight and now everything is instant and now everything is also very easy to cut.

Where the glass actually is
