HOW THE CARD WORKS, because I get asked this forty times a day. Nobody is judging whether Louella likes your jam. She is filling out a hundred-point card with four columns on it and the columns have fixed weights, and she has to write a number in each one whether she'd eat the jar or not. Flavor is forty points, and that is the biggest column, which is why a strawberry that tastes like strawberry can carry a jar a long way — but it is only forty. Texture & set is twenty-five. A soft spread that weeps a ring of syrup at the edge of the spoon loses in that column and it does not matter one bit that it was the best flavor on the table, because the flavor column is already full up at forty and there is nowhere else for those points to go. That's arithmetic, not opinion.

Before any lid comes off, every jar gets turned in the hand: seal, rim, headspace, ring rust, floating fruit, siphoning down the side. A chip or a smear of jam under the rim is a disqualification straight out, regardless of what's inside it, and we do that first so nobody's hopes get up. And I will say it again in print — PARAFFIN IS NOT ACCEPTED IN ANY CLASS AND HAS NOT BEEN SINCE THE RULES CHANGED. Two jars came in under wax this morning. They come off the table. Come see me and I'll show you the sheet.

Judges taste with a fresh spoon every single time and rinse between entries, which is why judging forty-one jams takes until eleven o'clock and why there is a coffee can of spoons the size of a mailbox behind the head table.

(On the pack: a whole dill class is half won before the lid comes off. Ardith stands the jar up, looks at it dead-on like a photograph, and if the cucumbers are all different lengths and one is bobbing at the top she has already written a number down. Cut them to the jar, not the jar to them. And an over-processed pickle cannot be hidden — she cuts one crosswise and it is either white and crisp all the way through or it is glassy and empty in the middle, and everybody standing at the rope can see it too.)

BEST OF SHOW, CANNED GOODS: ENTRY 106, DESSA KUHLMANN, STRAWBERRY PRESERVE, 96 POINTS. It is on the head table right now under the box fan with the rosette leaned against it, and it will sit there through Sunday. Two of the three judges independently wrote the word "clean" on their cards, which never happens.

Best of Show came down to a tie at 96 with the sour cherry, 221, and it broke on the flavotflavor flavor column — 39 to 37 — exactly the way the rule reads. Naomi took it beautifully and went and got a lemonade.
