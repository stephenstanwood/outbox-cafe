READ THIS BEFORE YOU SUGGEST ANYTHING

A hiccup is two separate events and everybody gets this wrong. First the diaphragm — the flat muscle under his lungs — pulls down all at once, nobody asked it to. Then about thirty-five thousandths of a second later the glottis, the door at the top of the windpipe, slams shut. The noise is the second one. The noise is the door, not the muscle. I had him hiccup with his mouth wide open by the bait cooler Thursday to settle an argument and it sounded exactly the same, so the mouth has nothing to do with it and neither does what he's saying at the time.

The signal runs down the phrenic nerve and back up the vagus, and both of those go right past his neck. Nothing above the collarbone gets a vote. That is why "just relax about it" is not a cure, it is a sentence, and I do not want to hear it again from the propane outfit.

Every cure that has ever worked on anybody does one of exactly two things: raises the carbon dioxide in the blood, or physically scratches the back of the throat. That is the entire list. If your idea doesn't do one of those two, it isn't a cure, it's conversation, and I've got leeches to bag.

His rate is 9.2 a minute and has been since day three. Rate holds steady inside a bout. It slows when the carbon dioxide comes up, which means the stopwatch can tell you a cure is working ten minutes before anybody in this shop has an opinion about it. Watch the gap column. The gap column doesn't like anybody.
