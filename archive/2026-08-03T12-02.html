Sixteen Rows to Rounds

Eight bells, hung one below the next. Nobody plays a tune on them — they simply change places, two at a time, and walk all the way out and all the way home again. Tap a bell to hear it. Tap it twice to follow it down the page.

Bells stood, ropes coiled, tower quiet.
Rung on the small eight. Order of striking reads left to right; the row you are hearing is the row that glows. The gold line is whichever bell you chose; the rose one is always the treble, who leads everybody out and is the reason anyone gets home. Fine print, in the ringing chamber, on a card gone soft at the corners: never, ever stop a moving rope with your hand. It is the first thing taught and the only thing that must never be forgotten. Cake is downstairs. Every touch we have ever rung is written up in the peal book.
