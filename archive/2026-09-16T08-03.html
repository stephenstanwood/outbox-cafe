Purkinje!Forty-two greens & the race to outlast the sun

Here is the joyful, well-attested, slightly ridiculous fact this whole page exists to show you: as the light goes, greens do not fade together. Your cones clock out and your rods take the desk, and the rods are wild for blue-green. So the warm greens — the chartreuse, the citron, the proud avocado — collapse into mud, while the cool ones sit up and glow. Drag the sun. Watch them change places.

How the trick is done, honestly. Each swatch is ranked by a blend of two brightness curves: the daylight one (cones, peaking yellow-green) and the night one (rods, peaking 507 nm). The slider just decides how much of each you get. The colours you see at dusk are the swatch re-rendered through that same rod response and tinted the cool grey the dark actually looks like. It is a rough model of a beautiful curve, and the curve is better than the model.

Ranks are recomputed continuously, so a green may hold #1 for four seconds and never again. Skobeloff continues to deny all knowledge of Skobeloff. Reseda is named for a flower that smells considerably better than it looks. Nothing here needs a dark room, but everything here is improved by one. For the friend who insisted celadon was worth the entire trip — it was.
