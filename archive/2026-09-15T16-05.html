What Goes In

Yield: enough to plug 4,096 fields.
  • 2 parts screen bloom, from any monitor that ran past midnight
  • 1 part tape shavings, quarter-inch, degaussed twice
  • 1 part letterhead pulp — pre-2000 rag stock only, the kind watermarked with a globe
  • 9 g pigment, pale lemon. Do not substitute.
  • Binder: slate, cold, whatever's in the bucket by the loading door

What You Do

  1. Mix in a room with no clock in it.
  2. Fold. Never stir — stirring makes it count.
  3. Rest nineteen minutes. Not twenty. Nineteen.
  4. It will pull itself to a mirror finish on its own. Let it. That's how you know it took.
  5. Press into the field with the flat of a butter knife; wipe the seam toward the year.
  6. Cure face-down. It reads ceilings.

If It Fails

  • Goes green → your pulp was minted after the rollover. Begin again; burn the batch.
  • Won't chrome → too much clock in the room. Check wrists. Check everyone's wrists.
  • Sets soft → the shavings still had something on them. That's fine. That's not your problem yet.
  • Tastes of anything at all → then you tasted it, and we did tell you, and it is in the log now.
