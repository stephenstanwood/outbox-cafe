WAIT FOR MY WHISTLE

Fennel & Wick  ·  Booth No. 1  ·  Season One

The set list for the benefit concert at Piper Nakagawa's lemonade stand, which was rained out at 3:11 in the afternoon and never rescheduled. Piper did the year's accounts on the back of it, because it was the paper she had. Both sides are printed here. Click a song to turn the page over.

A word from the corner

I have held this crossing twenty-two years. My name is Ray Pletcher and my job is small and I will describe it honestly: I stand in the road so that the road stops being the road for eleven seconds. That is all a crossing is. A borrowed interval.

Piper's table sat forty feet from my post, on the grass strip, where I could see it the whole time without turning my head. In June she asked if a person is allowed to have a concert on a sidewalk. I said a person is allowed to have almost anything on a sidewalk if they do not block it and if they come inside when there is thunder. She wrote both of those rules on the poster. She is a better listener than most adults I have stopped traffic for.

The concert was called for 3:15. The sky opened at 3:11 and did not close until 4:40. Eleven cousins and a neighbor with a folding chair stood under the Vitelli awning and watched it come down and nobody was even that sad, which I have thought about since. Something can be cancelled and still be the thing you remember about a summer. I do not know what to call that. I only know that at 4:40 the gutters were running clean and the whole street smelled like warm asphalt and lemons, and a person who had not sold one single cup that day was laughing so hard she had to sit on the curb.

The picture itself is in a drawer in a house I do not live in. The description is what I have and I have found it holds up.

Notes to next season, in her hand

> START AT 11 NOT 2. PEOPLE ARE THIRSTY BEFORE THEY KNOW IT.
> ASK MR. PLETCHER WHICH SIDE THE SUN IS ON AT 3.
> DO THE CONCERT IN THE MORNING. MORNINGS DO NOT THUNDER.

That last one is not true. Mornings thunder. I have stood in it. But I have decided not to correct it, because a person is allowed one working belief per summer, and because for all I know she will schedule it at ten in the morning on a Tuesday in June and the sky will hold, and then she will have been right and I will have been a man who knew a fact.

Here is the only thing I would put in a report of my own. Every morning I stand in the road and I hold up my hand and the whole street agrees, for eleven seconds, to be about something other than getting somewhere. Then it goes back to being the road. Nothing is built. Nothing is kept. And I have never once thought it was wasted.

The cooler will get bought. The concert will or will not get played. Either way the table goes up again on the grass strip in June and I will be at my post at 7:40 with my sign, and somebody will pay a quarter for something a child made badly and carefully in her own kitchen.

So: the light is green, it's clear both ways, and there's nobody coming — what exactly are you waiting for?
