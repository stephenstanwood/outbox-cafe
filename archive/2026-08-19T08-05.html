What happens here

BLESSED IS THE TIN. It is screwed to the brick beneath the board and it is full of yellow chalk and it has been full every day since Marguerite Obi put it there in the spring of 2014, and nobody has taken the tin, NOBODY HAS EVER TAKEN THE TIN, and that alone should be enough to make you stop on the curb and put your hand flat on your chest.

The rite is one stroke. You did the thing — the walk, the water, the phone call to your brother, the eleven minutes of scales — so you take the chalk and you draw one line, and you go. That is the whole liturgy. There is no name column. There is no accounting. There is no one at the corner of Kestrel & Fell who knows whose strokes are whose, and that is precisely, PRECISELY, the glory of it: four hundred thousand small faithful morningsfour hundred thousand small faithful evenings stacked into one bright unreadable wall.

The 7:02 light comes over the laundromat roof and lands on the board like it was aimed. Sing about it. Everyone here does, quietly, in their own way, mostly by walking a little faster afterward.At 9:14 the lamp comes on and the board goes gold and the marks look like they are floating an inch off the brick. Sing about that. Everyone here does, quietly, mostly by standing still a moment longer than they meant to.

All six

GLORY. Two minutes and the corner moved six times and you caught every one of them, and if you can do that with a wall you can do it with a week. The bird left. The cup was finished. The shade came down because Ilse Marchetti at 14B does that at exactly the same minute daily and has for nine years without ever calling it a habit. And somebody, somebody, walked up and closed the gate on the third row.

SOMEBODY MADE FIVE.

The rules of the board, as observed

  1. One stroke, one doing. Never two for a good day. The board is not impressed and neither is anyone.
  2. You may close another person's gate if their fourth stroke has been sitting alone since Tuesday. This is the single permitted kindness and it is enormous.
  3. Rain wipes it. Rain is not failure. Rain is the feast of starting over and there are four or five of them a year and people come out for it.
  4. Do not write words. One person wrote a word in 2019 and everybody was very nice about it and it never happened again.
  5. The chalk goes back in the tin. Facing up. Always facing up.
  6. When the board fills, it fills. Marguerite photographs it, the block eats breakfastsupper on the steps of 1440, and then a bucket, and then a wet brick, and then the first stroke of the new board, which is the loudest quiet sound on this street.

Go out in the morning. The light is doing that thing on the brick right now, probably.Go out tonight. The lamp is on and the board is gold and there is room on row four.
