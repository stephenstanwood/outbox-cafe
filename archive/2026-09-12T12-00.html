Twenty-six months now since the coil in B4 turned and nothing has replaced it except a fluorescent orange cheese product that has never once made anybody quieter.

So the Circle keeps the record. Not out of grief. Out of accuracy. Here follows the Sunwheel as it was, wheel by wheel, laid out the way it was meant to be eaten — top down, crown to root, the way the spiral unwound in the hand if the hand was patient.

SEVENTH WHEEL — THE HALO
crown · spun sugar lace

Barely a substance. A pale burnt-sugar web laid over everything else, gone in the mouth before a decision could be made about it. Members who ate upward missed it entirely and were, over the years, noticeably harder to talk to.

posted @ 05.02.2003 · 09:12 pm · by gwen_solarplexus

Somebody at the Thursday circle asked, kindly, whether a snack cake can really carry seven wheels of a body. Fair question. Here is the answer the Circle gives, which is not a clever answer:

It carried them for us. Sixty-five cents, a wet hallway, chlorine in the hair, and for the length of one spiral the whole stack of us lined up straight. Nothing else in that building did that. Not the sauna. Not the lane rope. Not the vending machine's other eleven slots, all of which are still there and all of which are still empty of it.

Fenmark ran the Sunwheel from 1988 to March 2001. Thirteen years, one wrapper design, one flavor, no seasonal variant, no fun size. That is the whole history of the thing and it is a good history.

A shelf in Wilkes-Barre, photographed hopefully, containing no Sunwheels.
